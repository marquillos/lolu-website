Why it matters

What residents run into

These are the barriers people with disabilities report most, and the ones we find most often on government websites.

Pages without a clear outline

Most screen reader users find information by jumping between headings. Missing or out-of-order headings leave them lost.

Errors shown only in red

Colorblind residents miss required fields and form errors marked by color alone, and can't finish an application or payment.

Meetings without captions

Deaf and hard-of-hearing residents are shut out of recorded council meetings when videos have no accurate captions.

Agendas locked in PDFs

Scanned or untagged PDFs can't be read aloud. Agendas, minutes and forms are often the biggest backlog.

How we work

Automated where it helps, human where it counts

Automated tools catch about half of real accessibility issues. The rest takes people who use the web the way your residents do.

  1. Scan every page

    We scan your whole site against WCAG 2.1 AA and list every linked document.

  2. Test by hand

    We use your key pages with a keyboard only, with screen readers (NVDA and VoiceOver), and at 400% zoom.

  3. Test with residents

    Paid Georgia testers who use assistive technology try your most important tasks. Panel launching fall 2026.

  4. Plan and track fixes

    You get plain-language fixes grouped by template, so one change clears many issues. Monitoring shows progress each month.

What we won't do

Sell overlay widgets. Disabled users report that they break screen readers. We fix the site itself.

Certify your site as "compliant." No one can. We review conformance and show measurable progress.

Use scare tactics. You'll get a realistic path, not a lawsuit threat.

FAQ

Common questions

Is the Title II deadline still in effect?

Yes. In April 2026 the Justice Department moved the dates to April 26, 2027 for governments of 50,000+ people, and April 26, 2028 for smaller governments and special districts. The standard, WCAG 2.1 AA, did not change. Existing ADA obligations still apply today.

Isn't an automated scan enough?

No. Automated tools find about half of real issues. Problems with keyboard use, screen reader flow, captions and documents need a person to test them.

Do you fix our website?

We tell your staff and web vendor exactly what to fix and in what order, then verify the fixes. Keeping remediation with your vendor keeps costs down and your site in your control.

Do you work with our web platform?

Yes. Our reports work with any platform, including CivicPlus, Granicus, Revize and WordPress. Many fixes are template changes your vendor can make once.

Can we pay by purchase order?

Yes. We accept purchase orders, ACH and checks, and provide a W-9 on request.

Are you a law firm?

No. We provide technical accessibility reviews. For legal questions, please consult your city or county attorney.
