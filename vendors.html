The rule that matters

Governments must meet WCAG 2.1 AA by April 2027. The software they buy counts.

The Department of Justice's ADA Title II web rule covers every state and local government. A government stays responsible for the web content and apps its vendors run for it, so before the deadline it will ask you for proof.

  • The standard: WCAG 2.1 Level AA, for web content and mobile apps.
  • The scope: web pages, PDFs, video, social posts, apps, and password-protected portals the government provides or operates.
  • The reach: outsourced tools count. Contracts now ask for warranties, indemnification and an ACR.

What is an ACR?

An ACR is a report card for one product, one criterion at a time

VPAT is the blank template, from the Information Technology Industry Council. ACR is the finished report. Here is how it plays out for a city and a vendor.

  1. 1

    The wall

    Ruth uses a screen reader. On her city's water-bill portal, the pay buttons have no labels. She hears “button, button, button” and gives up.

  2. 2

    The deadline

    Maple Springs has 62,000 residents. By April 26, 2027, its web content and mobile apps must meet WCAG 2.1 AA. That includes the portal a vendor runs for the city.

  3. 3

    The ask

    The city's ADA coordinator can't fix code she doesn't own. So she writes every vendor: “Please send us your ACR.”

  4. 4

    The question

    Dev, the vendor's founder, has never heard the term. An ACR is a finished VPAT: a report that rates the product, one WCAG criterion at a time.

  5. 5

    The test

    A tester checks five key flows with a scanner, a keyboard and a screen reader. The result is honest: some rows pass, some partly, a few fail.

  6. 6

    The result

    The city reads the ACR, sees the fix plan and signs. Dev fixes the gaps and re-tests every year. Ruth pays her bill in two minutes.

A fictional story. Maple Springs, Ruth and Dev are made up, but every step is how it really goes.

Glossary

Every acronym on this page, with the source behind it

Where a term comes from a law or a government agency, the link goes to that source. Standards and industry groups are labelled as such.

The law

ADAAmericans with Disabilities Act

The 1990 civil rights law that bars discrimination based on disability. Title II covers state and local governments.

  • Read the ADA text on ADA.gov (opens in a new tab)Government source
Title IIADA Title II

The part of the ADA about public services: cities, counties, school districts and other state and local government entities. Its regulations are what the web rule updates.

  • Title II regulations on ADA.gov (opens in a new tab)Government source
DOJU.S. Department of Justice

The federal agency that writes and enforces the ADA Title II regulations, including the web rule.

  • DOJ first steps for governments, on ADA.gov (opens in a new tab)Government source
Web ruleTitle II web and mobile accessibility rule

The DOJ's final rule of April 24, 2024 (89 FR 31320). It requires state and local governments to make web content and mobile apps meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA.

  • Final rule in the Federal Register (opens in a new tab)Government source
IFRInterim final rule

The April 2026 DOJ rule that extended the compliance dates by one year: April 26, 2027 and April 26, 2028. The DOJ has said it may revisit the rule.

  • Interim final rule in the Federal Register (opens in a new tab)Government source
CFRCode of Federal Regulations

Where federal rules are kept in final form. The web rule is 28 CFR Part 35, Subpart H, and section 35.200 sets the WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA requirement.

  • 28 CFR Part 35, Subpart H, on eCFR (opens in a new tab)Government source
Section 508Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act

The federal law that requires federal agencies to make their information and communication technology accessible. It is the origin of the VPAT and ACR habit in buying.

  • Section 508 laws and policies (opens in a new tab)Government source
  • Revised 508 Standards, U.S. Access Board (opens in a new tab)Government source

The standards

WCAGWeb Content Accessibility Guidelines

The technical standard behind the rule. It has success criteria at three levels, A, AA and AAA. The DOJ rule asks for 2.1 Levels A and AA, which is 50 criteria.

  • WCAG 2.1 from the W3C (opens in a new tab)Standards body
  • Where the rule requires it, 28 CFR 35.200 (opens in a new tab)Government source
W3CWorld Wide Web Consortium

The international standards body that publishes WCAG.

  • W3C home page (opens in a new tab)Standards body
ICTInformation and communication technology

The word federal accessibility law uses for software, websites, hardware, electronic content and support documents.

  • ICT in an ACR, on Section508.gov (opens in a new tab)Government source
EN 301 549European accessibility standard for ICT

The European standard that sets accessibility requirements for ICT. It is why the International edition adds a mapping for EU and UK buyers.

  • EU web accessibility policy (opens in a new tab)Government source

The report

VPATVoluntary Product Accessibility Template

The blank template, created by the Information Technology Industry Council. It translates standards such as Section 508, EN 301 549 and WCAG into rows to test.

  • VPAT from the Information Technology Industry Council (opens in a new tab)Industry group
ACRAccessibility Conformance Report

A finished VPAT. It explains how one product conforms to an accessibility standard, which is what government buyers ask vendors for.

  • What an ACR is, on Section508.gov (opens in a new tab)Government source

Questions

What vendors ask first

Is an ACR a certification?

No. It documents how your product conforms to WCAG on the day it was tested. It is not a legal opinion, and nobody can certify a product as compliant.

What if you find serious problems?

The report says so plainly, with evidence and a suggested fix for each. You decide what to publish. Buyers trust a report that shows gaps and a plan more than one with every row marked Supports.

What do you need from me?

A test account, the five flows you want covered, and the product URL. Create a dedicated test user and keep real customer data out of it.

Do you test native mobile apps?

Not yet. This service covers web applications and websites.

Do you fix the issues too?

No. We write the report and recommend fixes. Your team makes them, and the yearly re-test updates the report afterward.

Can I share the report with customers?

Yes. It is yours to publish and attach to bids.
